VIX Health System

What
Hospital management platform for a private network in Trento
Team
4 people: V. Fraccaroli, A. Harti, N. Kaur, me
My share
66 of 129 commits, the most on the team; repository admin
Stack
Java, Spring Boot 4, Thymeleaf, JPA, H2, Firebase, Doxygen

A public website (departments, specialists, an interactive hospital map, a questionnaire that points patients to the right specialist) in front of six role portals: Patient, Medical Specialist, Secretary, Technician, Buyer and Staff Manager. Patients book and pay for appointments; staff manage records, surgeries, shifts, vacations, machines and inventory, and every action lands in an audit log.

The architecture is a Rich Domain Model: business rules live on the entities themselves (a patient makes an appointment, an appointment cancels itself) and services only orchestrate and persist. I led a full audit that brought every model, service and controller in line with that rule.

What I built

  • Staff portalsTechnician, Staff Manager and Medical Specialist areas: machine alerts and repair history, employee creation, shifts and vacations with conflict checks, a monthly calendar and daily schedule for specialists.
  • Front endRebuilt the Thymeleaf templates around real controller data only, with shared fragments and a crimson-and-gold design system.
  • Domain refactorImplemented missing domain methods, moved inline logic out of controllers, wired type-safe enums for every status field.
  • PersistenceMoved to a file-based H2 database in PostgreSQL mode with idempotent seed data, so the team's SQL stayed portable and data survived restarts.
  • FeaturesSimulated SPID/CIE patient login, redirect-after-login, opening-hours enforcement, room transfer confirmation, resource withdrawal tracking with history.
  • Testing & docsModel and service test suite; Doxygen documentation with a custom dark theme and diagrams, published on GitHub Pages.
  • DeliverablesWrote the Functional Requirements and the architecture, language choice and dependency sections of the final report.

Bipolar Orchestrator

What
Concurrent space simulation: 7 planets, 2 explorers, one two-minded orchestrator
Team
2 people: Viviana Fraccaroli and me
My share
19 of 22 commits; the whole GUI and all of its pixel art
Stack
Rust, Bevy 0.19, crossbeam channels, Aseprite

The course fixed the world: planets written by other student groups, explorers, sunrays and asteroids, all talking over a shared message protocol. Each team designed its own orchestrator. Ours is one body with two minds. Solace nurtures planets with sunrays; Eclipse destroys them with asteroids. Each believes the other is the villain, and each keeps waking the other up.

Mechanically, a global hostility value climbs over about four minutes and hands control to Eclipse once it crosses one half. Every planet also gets a randomly assigned curve (sine, cosine, sawtooth, triangle, square or exponential) that makes its own odds wobble, blended against its inverse by hostility. A death eases the pressure a little. The player can intervene, framed as infiltrating the orchestrator's mind, but repeated overrides against a personality's nature raise a suspicion meter until she refuses.

What I built

  • gui/The full Bevy interface: a first-person spaceship cockpit around the galaxy, animated planets and explorers, portrait and stance for whichever mind is in control, sunray and asteroid projectiles, holographic inspect panels, a map hologram, resource badges, suspicion meters and a game-over screen.
  • Pixel artEvery sprite sheet drawn in Aseprite: 7 planets with idle, hit, dying and destroyed states, Solace and Eclipse, both explorers, nebulas, the cockpit, buttons and cursor. Animation timing is read from the Aseprite JSON at startup, never hardcoded.
  • probability/The curve system, the hostility ramp, a grace period for newborn planets, and the Solace/Eclipse rules.
  • galaxy/, routing/Topology parser, explorer movement protocol, the factories for all seven planet crates, and the interactive CLI.
  • explorer_jebediahCo-wrote our second explorer, inspired by Jebediah Kerman: I built his AI state machine and his mapping of the galaxy. He is the reckless cartographer.

Problems worth telling

A message eaten by the wrong reader

Several callers shared one channel and accepted whatever arrived next as their acknowledgement. A leftover message from a dying planet could be read as another planet's reply. A matching helper now discards strays until the real acknowledgement arrives.

Galaxies dying in three seconds

A planet that has never received a sunray cannot build a rocket, so its first asteroid is always fatal. With seven planets rolling at once, someone died on tick one. A short grace period for new planets fixed it; games went from seconds to minutes.

The screen must never lie

Our rule was that the GUI only shows what the backend confirms. Explorer movement is driven by a real move event queued per explorer, not guessed on the client, and the simulation waits for the window before starting.

Multi-process Blockchain

What
A blockchain run as cooperating Unix processes
Team
3 people: V. Fraccaroli, A. Harti, me
My share
Process lifecycle, IPC and the Miner; about 3,400 lines
Stack
C11, POSIX, pthreads, Unix domain sockets, Bash, Make

Clients generate transactions, Miners collect them and race to produce a block, and Node 0 validates and appends the winner before broadcasting it to the other nodes, which recover any blocks they missed. Blocks carry SHA-256 hashes and Merkle roots, the chain is saved as CSV, and a separate Bash script can verify it independently. The point of the project was to exercise the operating system underneath it.

What I built

  • bootstrap.cStarts and manages everything: argument parsing, the runtime directory, fork and exec for every child, PID tracking, reaping crashed children, and the operator console (submit, request, save, pause, resume, stop).
  • miner.c, mempool.cA communication thread and a mining thread over a mutex-protected mempool. Mining sleeps can be interrupted the moment another block is committed or shutdown starts, and a lagging miner resyncs from Node 0.
  • ipc.c, process_info.cThe Unix domain socket layer every process uses, with framing, size limits and tests, plus shared process bookkeeping and the new IPC and process error codes.
  • tests/, scripts/Integration tests for bootstrap, miner restart, pause/resume, crash handling, shutdown and cleanup, plus demo, cleanup and environment-check scripts wired into the Makefile.
Double free under load

An uninitialized block struct in the miner's commit and resync path corrupted the heap once real blocks started flowing. Found it in a live run, fixed it, and made the crash test actually crash a process.
